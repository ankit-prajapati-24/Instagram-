Every beat, before the footage is fetched

Listen to each one. To say a beat yourself, copy its line into whatever voice you want to use, then upload what comes back — mp3, wav, m4a, ogg, flac, or the video you recorded on a phone. It is re-encoded to match the rest of the narration and levelled to the same loudness, so an uploaded beat does not jump out next to a synthesised one. Replacing a beat changes how long the video is, which is what the line below tracks.

If the voice simply says a word wrong, you do not need a recording at all: fix the spelling in the spoken line and press Say it again. Only that beat is spoken again. The two lines move independently on purpose — respell the word phonetically in the spoken line and the burned caption keeps the real spelling, so the listener hears it right and the viewer reads it right. Editing only the caption re-times the words without saying anything again.

Every clip, before it is rendered

One card per clip, not per beat — a beat holds several. The query underneath each one is what the search was asked for, which is usually why a wrong clip is wrong. Pick a replacement video or image for any slot; its length does not matter, because the slot is fixed by the narration and the render trims or loops to it. A still gets the Ken Burns move automatically.
